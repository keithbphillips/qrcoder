Print Calibration

Eight QR panels, all the same physical size, at descending module sizes. Each says out loud what it is, in plain text, so your phone's ordinary camera reads them with no app at all. Panels are now sized to the real bookmark panel, and the PDF carries its own geometry so nothing can rescale it. Two sheets, four panels each, so nothing lands on a page boundary.

  1. Use the PDF and print at 100% / "actual size" — never "fit to page".
  2. Open the normal camera app and point it at each panel, largest modules first.
  3. Each readable panel shows a line of text naming its version and module size.
  4. Note the smallest one that still reads. That is your ceiling.
  5. Two sheets print. The second holds the four finest panels.

Earlier prints of this sheet were oversized. The page drew 2.20 in panels while computing every label for a 1.985 in bookmark panel, so a browser print came out 10.8% large and each label understated the real module by the same margin: a panel labelled 0.379 mm actually printed at 0.420 mm. Screen, print and PDF now all use 1.985 in, so a label finally means what it says. If you tested before this note, re-test — your true limit is coarser than the old labels suggested.
Why a PDF. Browser printing rescales silently, and a few percent of scale is the difference between a module that reads and one that does not. The PDF states its own geometry in points, so what you measure is what reaches the paper. The codes are vector rectangles, not an image, so they stay sharp at any zoom.
